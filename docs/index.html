Acoustic UAV Detection on Edge Devices

A completed multi-dataset study of acoustic UAV detection using pretrained audio embeddings, supervised and unsupervised classifiers, cross-dataset transfer, and a compact TinyML deployment model.

Final study: six datasets — three public benchmarks and three field datasets collected by the team — evaluated under one protocol, with a separate full-int8 mini-SE-Net for edge deployment.

Project overview

One technical question, evaluated from several angles.

The project combines field data, public audio corpora, representation learning, supervised and anomaly detection, transfer evaluation, and deployment constraints rather than treating accuracy as the only goal.

01 / PROBLEM

Low-flying UAV detection

Study passive acoustic sensing as a local complementary detection layer.

02 / GENERALIZATION

Across datasets

Measure how learned UAV acoustics transfer between different corpora and platform families.

03 / MODELLING

Multiple approaches

Compare AST, YAMNet, Wav2Vec2, triplet adaptation, logistic regression, OCSVM, Isolation Forest, CNN, and teacher–student baselines.

04 / DEPLOYMENT

Edge constraints

Compare the high-quality embedding ceiling with a tiny quantized waveform model suitable for edge hardware.

Operational motivation

Why acoustic sensing is worth investigating.

Small, low-flying or radio-silent aircraft can be difficult to detect consistently with any single sensing method. The project studies microphones as a complementary sensor, not as a replacement for radar or air-defense systems.

Passive and local

Microphones are inexpensive, unobtrusive, and can process sound locally. Rotor noise also contains characteristic acoustic structure that can be exploited at short range.

Research goal
Find the strongest acoustic representation, test label-free detection, and measure what performance is still possible in a ~22 KB edge model.
System design

From field audio to a detection score.

Every dataset uses one-second 16 kHz clips so representations and classifiers can be compared under the same protocol.

01

Ingest

Combine three public corpora with Electric UAVs, FPV, and UAV field recordings.

02

Prepare

Segment audio, normalize sample rate, label UAV/non-UAV, and create train/validation/test splits.

03

Represent

Extract AST, YAMNet, Wav2Vec2, triplet-adapted embeddings, or log-mel features.

04

Detect

Train logistic regression, One-Class SVM, Isolation Forest, CNN, and teacher–student models.

05

Deploy

Train the raw-waveform mini-SE-Net and quantize it to full int8 TFLite.

Final evaluation protocol

The completed benchmark answers three different questions.

All configurations share the same dataset splits and metrics, making the comparison primarily about representation, supervision level, transfer, and deployment cost.

Completed

Six-dataset benchmark

Supervised and unsupervised detectors are evaluated on six corpora with accuracy, F1, AUC, AP, training time, and inference latency.

Completed

Triplet adaptation

Every embedding/classifier pair is compared with its triplet-adapted counterpart to measure when domain adaptation helps.

Completed

Cross-dataset transfer

Models are trained on the other corpora and tested on FPV to measure whether UAV acoustics generalize across datasets.

Final results

Pretrained embeddings set a strong quality ceiling.

Linear probes on AST or YAMNet win on every single dataset. Public corpora are close to ceiling; the team’s field recordings are harder, with UAV the most difficult dataset.

Triplet fine-tuning

Adaptation helps where the domain is harder.

Triplet fine-tuning is dataset-dependent. It helps most consistently on the team’s field recordings and on cross-dataset transfer, while often hurting on the easier public corpora.

FIELD DATA

Electric UAVs + UAV

Triplet variants improve 8 of 9 encoder/classifier combinations on each dataset, with mean AUC gains of about +0.15 and +0.17.

FPV

Big unsupervised lift

AST-triplet raises Isolation Forest from 0.483 to 0.911 and OCSVM from 0.554 to 0.895.

PUBLIC DATA

Often unnecessary

Triplet adaptation degrades most combinations on Binary Drone Audio and all nine combinations on Multiclass.

TRANSFER

All nine improve

In the pooled cross-dataset run, every triplet variant improves over its frozen counterpart.

Edge deployment

The final TinyML model fits in about 22.6 KB.

The full-int8 mini-SE-Net consumes raw one-second waveforms directly. It is dramatically smaller than the pretrained embedding pipelines, but gives up accuracy on the hardest field datasets.

Deployment profile

The model has roughly 7.4k parameters and was post-training quantized to full int8 TFLite.

  • Input16,000 samples
  • Model size~22.6 KB
  • Parameters~7.4k
  • Host CPU latency~1.5–2.5 ms

Important limitation

The final latency measurements were made with the TFLite interpreter on a Colab host CPU, not on the target embedded device. A physical-device benchmark remains future work.
