Acoustic UAV Detection with Pretrained Audio Embeddings

A Multi-Dataset Benchmark and an Edge-Deployable Detector

Roei Yanku · Melissa Liebowitz · Tal Kfir · Or Anidjar · Boaz Ben-Moshe
Department of Computer Science, Ariel University, Ariel, Israel

← Back to project website

Abstract

Passive acoustic sensing is a low-cost, unobtrusive way to detect small unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs). We benchmark frozen pretrained audio embeddings — AST, YAMNet, and Wav2Vec 2.0, with and without triplet fine-tuning — combined with supervised and unsupervised classifiers, plus log-mel CNN and teacher–student baselines, on six UAV audio datasets: three public benchmarks and three field datasets collected by the team. Supervised linear probes on AST/YAMNet embeddings dominate, unsupervised detectors remain competitive on most corpora, and triplet fine-tuning helps mainly on field recordings and cross-dataset transfer. A deployable full-int8 mini-SE-Net of roughly 7.4k parameters and ~22.6 KB reaches AUC 0.68–0.99 across the single datasets.

1. Introduction

Small UAVs are increasingly cheap and capable, raising safety, privacy, and security concerns. Radar and RF systems dominate commercial counter-UAV deployments, but small, autonomous, or radio-silent platforms remain challenging. Passive acoustic detection is a useful complement because microphones are inexpensive and rotor noise provides a characteristic signal at short range.

The study asks three practical questions: which models detect UAVs most accurately, which are light enough for edge devices, and how well the methods work on real field recordings rather than only curated public data.

3. Method

Each clip is represented using AST, YAMNet, or Wav2Vec 2.0 embeddings. Each encoder is also evaluated after triplet-loss fine-tuning. Classifiers include logistic regression, one-class SVM, Isolation Forest, a log-mel CNN, and a teacher–student anomaly-detection baseline. The TinyML track uses a raw-waveform mini-SE-Net quantized to full int8 TFLite.

5. Supervised vs. unsupervised

The best unsupervised detector stays close to the supervised probe on the field datasets: Electric UAVs 0.904 vs. 0.913 AUC, FPV 0.911 vs. 0.922, and UAV 0.747 vs. 0.759. This suggests strong embeddings can make label-free anomaly detection practical in some settings.

6. Triplet fine-tuning

The benefit is dataset-dependent. Triplet variants improve 8 of 9 combinations on both Electric UAVs and UAV, and all nine combinations in the pooled cross-dataset experiment. On FPV, AST-triplet strongly improves the unsupervised detectors. On the easier public corpora, adaptation is often unnecessary or harmful.

8. Conclusion

Frozen AST and YAMNet embeddings with linear probes achieve AUC 0.759–1.000 across the six datasets. Unsupervised detectors remain competitive on strong embeddings, and triplet fine-tuning helps most consistently on the team’s field recordings and cross-dataset transfer. The int8 mini-SE-Net reaches AUC 0.68–0.99 on the single datasets while fitting in about 22.6 KB.
